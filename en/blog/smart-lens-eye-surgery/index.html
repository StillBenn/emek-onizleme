What Is Smart Lens Eye Surgery? Is More Comfortable Vision Possible at Far, Near and Intermediate Distances?

Eye health is one of the most important factors directly affecting everyday quality of life. Cataract and difficulty seeing close up, which can develop particularly with age, can make people's daily activities harder. Thanks to today's advancing intraocular lens technologies, there are different treatment options for suitable patients that aim to support vision at more than one distance.

One of these options is multifocal intraocular lenses, commonly known as smart lenses. These special lenses, which can be placed in the eye in place of the natural lens during cataract surgery, are planned according to the patient's vision needs.

Op. Dr. Tayyar Karadağ, Ophthalmology Specialist at Emek Hospital Gaziantep, draws attention to the importance of correct patient selection and a detailed eye examination in smart lens procedures.

What Is a Smart Lens?

A smart lens is a special artificial intraocular lens, designed to support vision at different distances, that is placed inside the eye after the eye's natural lens has been removed.

Used particularly in cataract operations, these lenses may help to meet the need for vision at far, intermediate and near distances, depending on their optical design.

The lenses called smart lenses do not work with an electronic or digital system. The name is used because of their advanced optical features, which support focusing at more than one distance.

What Does a Smart Lens Do?

The main aim of smart lenses is to improve the quality of vision by taking the place of the natural lens that has lost its transparency because of cataract and, with suitable lens types, to help reduce the need for glasses.

Depending on the lens design, the following advantages may be aimed for:

  • Distance vision: May support activities such as watching television, driving and seeing your surroundings.
  • Intermediate vision: May provide more comfortable vision when using a computer, preparing food and doing everyday tasks.
  • Near vision: May reduce the need for glasses when reading books, using a phone and doing close work.
  • Reduced dependence on glasses: May reduce the need to wear glasses at different distances in suitable patients.

However, it cannot be guaranteed that the need for glasses will disappear completely. The visual outcome may vary depending on the type of lens used, the structure of the eye and any existing eye diseases the person has.

What Are the Types of Smart Lens?

Intraocular lens technologies are designed according to different vision needs. The working principle and the range of vision offered are not the same for every lens.

Trifocal (Three-Focus) Lenses

Trifocal lenses are designed to support vision at far, intermediate and near distances. They may be considered particularly in people who wish to reduce their need for glasses at different distances and whose eye structure is suitable.

Multifocal Lenses

Multifocal lenses aim to provide vision at different distances by directing light to more than one focal point. Depending on the lens's optical design, intermediate vision as well as near and distance vision may be supported.

EDOF (Extended Depth of Focus) Lenses

EDOF lenses aim to provide a wider range of vision, particularly at far and intermediate distances. With some designs, additional glasses may still be needed for near vision. Compared with multifocal lenses, they may have different characteristics in terms of light reflections and halos.

Toric Lenses

Toric lenses are designed to help correct the refractive error of corneal origin known as astigmatism. For suitable patients, toric lens options with different focus characteristics may also be available.

Which lens will be used is determined in line with the person's eye measurements and visual expectations.

How Is Smart Lens Surgery Performed?

Smart lens surgery is a procedure mostly carried out as part of cataract surgery.

Before surgery, a detailed examination of the eye is carried out. Factors such as the structure of the cornea, the length of the eye, the health of the retina and macula, eye pressure and astigmatism are assessed. The person's vision needs in daily life and their expectations regarding the use of glasses are also established.

During surgery, the natural lens that has lost its transparency is removed, generally through a small incision. The artificial intraocular lens chosen in advance is then placed inside the eye.

The procedure is mostly carried out with anaesthetic drops or another method of anaesthesia considered appropriate. The length of the operation and the recovery period may vary according to the structure of the patient's eye and the surgical conditions.

Who Is Smart Lens Surgery Suitable For?

The smart lens procedure may be considered particularly in people who have cataract and who wish to reduce their need for glasses at different distances through a suitable choice of lens.

However, this method is not suitable for every patient. The following factors are important in choosing a lens:

  • The degree of cataract and the general state of eye health
  • The health of the retina and macula
  • The presence of accompanying eye diseases such as glaucoma
  • The structure of the cornea and any astigmatism
  • The person's vision needs in daily life
  • How often the person drives at night and their sensitivity to light
  • Their expectations regarding reducing the use of glasses

The suitability of multifocal lenses should be assessed carefully, particularly in people with retinal diseases, advanced glaucoma or corneal problems.

What Are the Advantages of Smart Lens Surgery?

With correct patient selection, smart lenses may help to improve visual comfort at different distances in daily life.

Their key potential advantages are as follows:

  • They may support vision at far, intermediate and near distances
  • They may reduce the need for glasses in suitable patients
  • They help to relieve the blurred vision caused by cataract
  • They aim to improve visual comfort in daily activities
  • In some patients with astigmatism, the refractive error may also be corrected with toric options

The degree of these advantages depends on the design of the lens used and the characteristics of the person's eyes.

Does Smart Lens Surgery Have Risks?

Like other eye operations, smart lens surgery carries certain risks. After the operation, temporary blurred vision, a pricking sensation in the eye, sensitivity to light and dryness may occur.

Particularly with multifocal lenses, some patients may notice rings around lights at night (halos), light scatter or glare. Reduced contrast sensitivity can also occur with some lens designs.

Infection, bleeding inside the eye, retinal problems and changes in the pressure inside the eye are among the more serious but less common complications of cataract surgery.

For this reason, the possible benefits and risks should be explained in detail by the eye doctor before surgery.

Recovery After Smart Lens Surgery

The way vision clears after surgery may vary from person to person. While vision improves markedly in a short time in some patients, the adaptation and recovery period may be longer in others.

During the recovery period:

  • The eye drops prescribed by the doctor should be used regularly.
  • The eye should not be rubbed and should be protected from knocks.
  • Follow-up examinations should not be missed.
  • Heavy physical activity should be avoided for the period recommended by the doctor.
  • In the event of symptoms such as sudden loss of vision, severe pain, marked redness or flashes of light, an eye doctor should be consulted without delay.

Post-operative care and check-ups are important for a successful recovery.

Can a Smart Lens Be Used for Every Cataract Patient?

No. Smart lenses may not be the most appropriate option for every cataract patient. In some patients, standard single-focus lenses may be a more suitable choice.

For example, the lens should be chosen with greater care in people who have retinal or macular disease, significant optic nerve damage or high sensitivity to light reflections at night.

What matters here is not only reducing the need to wear glasses but also identifying the lens best suited to the person's eye health and lifestyle.

Specialist Assessment for Your Eye Health at Emek Hospital

For a successful result in smart lens procedures, a detailed eye examination, accurate measurements and a personalised choice of lens are very important.
