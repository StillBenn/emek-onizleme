What Is Salmon DNA? What Does It Do in Skin Care?

Over time, skin can lose its moisture, elasticity and natural radiance. Factors such as sunlight, environmental influences, ageing and lifestyle can cause the skin to look more tired and dull. One of the procedures aimed at supporting skin quality is the Salmon DNA procedure, which has attracted interest in the field of aesthetic dermatology in recent years.

Spec. Dr. Çağlar Soysal, Dermatology Specialist at Emek Hospital Gaziantep, emphasises that whether the Salmon DNA procedure is suitable for a person should be determined by assessing their skin structure and needs.

What Is Salmon DNA?

Salmon DNA is a general name for the purified DNA derivatives (products containing polynucleotide or PDRN) that are obtained from salmon and used in the field of aesthetic dermatology.

These products are classed among biostimulant procedures aimed at supporting the renewal processes of skin tissue. It is thought that they may have positive effects on the skin's moisture balance, elasticity and tissue quality.

The Salmon DNA procedure does not mean that new DNA is transferred into the skin or that the person's genetic make-up is changed. The aim of the procedure is to support the skin's natural renewal processes.

What Does Salmon DNA Do?

The Salmon DNA procedure may be considered in suitable people with the aim of supporting skin quality. The potential effects highlighted in research are as follows:

  • May support the skin's moisture balance: May contribute to a more hydrated and vibrant skin appearance.
  • May help to improve skin texture: May support a smoother-looking skin surface.
  • May contribute to preserving elasticity: It is among the procedures aimed at making the skin look firmer and more supple.
  • May help to reduce the appearance of fine lines: It may be considered particularly in individual care plans aimed at changes in skin quality.
  • May support a brighter appearance: May contribute to improving the overall appearance of the skin.

These effects may not be seen to the same degree in every person. Although scientific studies on the Salmon DNA procedure are promising, it should be remembered that the available evidence is still limited and that more comprehensive research is needed.

Which Areas Can Salmon DNA Be Applied To?

Depending on the properties of the product used and the person's needs, the Salmon DNA procedure may be considered on different areas of the skin.

The areas most often addressed are as follows:

  • Face area
  • Around the eyes
  • Neck
  • Décolletage area
  • Backs of the hands

The skin structure and anatomical features of each area are different. For this reason, the area to be treated, the product and the method should be determined through an assessment by a dermatology specialist.

How Is the Salmon DNA Procedure Performed?

The Salmon DNA procedure is generally carried out by injection into the skin or under the skin, according to the instructions for the product used.

Before the procedure, a dermatology specialist assesses the skin structure, expectations, existing skin problems and the person's medical history. The treatment area is cleaned and, if considered necessary, a local anaesthetic product may be used.

The appropriate product is then applied according to the areas and technique decided upon. The length of the procedure may vary depending on the area treated and the method used.

The number of sessions and the intervals between them are not the same for everyone. An individual treatment plan is drawn up according to the person's skin needs, the properties of the product used and the doctor's assessment.

Does the Salmon DNA Procedure Have Side Effects?

As with any injection procedure, some side effects may be seen with the Salmon DNA procedure too. Temporarily, at the application site:

  • Redness
  • Mild swelling
  • Tenderness
  • Bruising
  • Small injection bumps

may occur.

More serious reactions or complications such as infection are also among the possible risks of injection procedures. For this reason, it is important that the procedure is performed in appropriate healthcare conditions, by competent healthcare professionals and using the correct product.

Before the procedure, allergies, medicines being taken, existing diseases, and any pregnancy or breastfeeding must always be shared with the doctor.

Who May Salmon DNA Be Suitable For?

For people who wish to support their skin's moisture balance and overall appearance and who notice changes in their skin quality, the Salmon DNA procedure may be considered as part of a dermatological assessment.

However, this procedure is not suitable for everyone. Factors such as an active infection or inflammation of the skin, a known sensitivity to the contents of the product and the person's general state of health should be taken into account.

A dermatology specialist should decide whether the procedure is necessary and whether it will meet the person's expectations.

What Should Be Considered After Salmon DNA?

After the procedure, it is important to follow the doctor's recommendations. In general:

  • The application site should not be touched unnecessarily.
  • The skin should be cleaned in the way recommended by the doctor.
  • Care should be taken to protect against the sun.
  • Intense exercise, saunas and very hot environments should be avoided for the period recommended by the doctor.
  • If more pain than expected, increasing redness, marked swelling or anything else unusual develops at the application site, a healthcare facility should be contacted.

Aftercare recommendations may vary depending on the product used and the person's skin characteristics.

Why Is Specialist Assessment Important in the Salmon DNA Procedure?

Skin structure, age, the skin's moisture level, elasticity and expectations differ from one person to another. For this reason, it is not right to apply the same treatment plan to everyone.

In the Salmon DNA procedure, the contents of the product to be used, the application site, the session plan and the possible risks should be assessed by a dermatology specialist. It should also be understood that this procedure does not replace skin care, sun protection or healthy lifestyle habits.

Spec. Dr. Çağlar Soysal, Dermatology Specialist at Emek Hospital Gaziantep, states that the suitability of the Salmon DNA procedure should be assessed in line with the person's skin structure and needs.
